Did You Know? Indian Currency Facts

₹1 Note: It is the only currency note issued by the Government of India and signed by the Finance Secretary, not the RBI Governor.
₹0 Note: An organisation called 5th Pillar printed zero rupee notes as a protest tool against bribery.
Pre-2005 Notes: Notes with no printing year on the back were withdrawn from circulation by the RBI in 2014. Exchange them at a bank branch rather than passing them on.
Material: Indian notes are not ordinary paper. They are printed on cotton-based paper, which is why they survive a wash.

A Closing-Time Cash Routine That Catches Mistakes

  1. Fix an opening float. Start each day with a known amount of change (a set number of ₹10, ₹20 and ₹50 notes plus coins) and write it down. Closing cash minus the float is what you actually took in.
  2. Sort before you count. Face all notes the same way and stack by denomination. Count each stack by hand, then enter the number of pieces here, not the value, so the multiplication cannot go wrong.
  3. Compare with sales. Cash sales for the day should equal total billed sales minus card, UPI and credit sales. The counter total minus the float should match that figure.
  4. Log the difference. Record any shortage or excess with the date, the amount and the likely reason (wrong change, an unbilled sale, a note refused as suspect). A running log shows patterns that a single day cannot.
  5. Prepare the deposit. Bank pay-in slips ask for the number of notes of each denomination. The breakdown you entered above goes straight onto the slip, so fill the slip before you bundle the notes.
  6. Move cash off the premises. Deposit often and keep only the next day's float overnight. Shop insurance policies usually cap the cash they cover, so check your policy schedule.

The cash handling guide covers till discipline, staff handovers and petty cash in more detail. For the day's total in words on a voucher or receipt, paste the amount into the number to words converter.

Cash Rules Every Indian Business Should Know (as of September 2026)

Income tax law limits how much business can be done in cash. These limits were set under Sections 269ST, 40A(3), 269SS and 269T of the Income-tax Act, 1961 and carry forward under the Income-tax Act, 2025, which replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 and renumbered its sections, so look up the current section number before you cite one.

Rule Limit What it means at the counter
Cash receipts₹2 lakh or moreYou cannot accept ₹2 lakh or more in cash from one person in a day, for one transaction, or for one event or occasion, even in instalments. The penalty equals the amount received.
Cash expensesAbove ₹10,000 per person per dayBusiness payments in cash above this limit are disallowed as an expense (the limit is ₹35,000 for payments to transporters). Pay suppliers by cheque, transfer or UPI.
Cash loans and deposits₹20,000 or moreLoans, deposits and advances against property cannot be accepted or repaid in cash at this level. The penalty equals the amount.
PAN on large salesAbove ₹2 lakh per transactionThe customer's PAN must be recorded for any single sale or purchase of goods or services above ₹2 lakh, whatever the payment method.
Bank reporting₹10 lakh (savings) or ₹50 lakh (current) in a yearBanks report cash deposits that cross these totals in a financial year to the Income Tax Department, so keep sales records that explain your deposits.

These are limits, not advice. A chartered accountant can tell you how they apply to your business, and GST rules on invoicing apply to cash sales in the same way as to any other sale.

Spotting a Counterfeit Note at the Counter

The RBI publishes the security features of every denomination on its website. These are the ones you can check in a second or two without equipment:

  • Watermark: hold the note to the light to see the Mahatma Gandhi portrait and the denomination numeral in the blank area.
  • Security thread: a windowed thread with the words भारत and RBI runs through the note; on the higher denominations it changes colour when you tilt the note.
  • See-through register: the denomination numeral is printed in halves on the two sides and forms a complete number against the light.
  • Raised printing: on the higher denominations the portrait, the RBI seal and the identification mark near the edge are printed in intaglio and can be felt with a fingertip.
  • Number panel: in the current series the numerals grow from small to large from left to right.
  • Micro lettering: RBI and the denomination value appear in tiny letters near the portrait, visible with a magnifying glass.

Under RBI rules a bank that receives a counterfeit note impounds it, stamps it and gives you an acknowledgement; you do not get its value back. That is why checking large notes when they are tendered matters more than any check at the bank. A small UV lamp is a cheap addition to a busy counter: genuine notes have optical fibres and number panels that glow under it, and a note that stays dull deserves a closer look.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Indian currency notes are in circulation in 2026?

The RBI issues ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹200 and ₹500 notes. The ₹2,000 note was withdrawn from circulation in May 2023 but remains legal tender, and older ₹1, ₹2 and ₹5 notes are still valid even though they are no longer printed in volume.

Is the ₹2,000 note still valid?

Yes. The RBI withdrew it from circulation on 19 May 2023, but it continues to be legal tender. Banks stopped exchanging it on 7 October 2023, and it can now be exchanged or deposited through RBI issue offices under the RBI's current rules.

Are 50 paise coins still legal tender?

Yes. Coins of 50 paise remain legal tender. Coins of 25 paise and below were withdrawn from 30 June 2011 and are no longer accepted.

How much cash can a shop accept from one customer?

Receiving ₹2 lakh or more in cash from one person in a day, for one transaction, or for one event is prohibited under income tax law, and the penalty equals the amount received. Below that limit cash sales are allowed, but the customer's PAN must be recorded for any single sale above ₹2 lakh whatever the payment method.

How do shopkeepers count cash at the end of the day?

Sort the notes by denomination, count each stack, and multiply by the face value. Enter those counts here to get the total instantly, compare it with the day's cash sales, and note any shortage or excess before locking up.

Does this cash counter store my counts?

Counts are saved only in your own browser so they survive a page refresh. Nothing is sent to a server, and Reset All clears the saved values.

Can I use this counter to fill a bank deposit slip?

Yes. Pay-in slips ask for the number of notes of each denomination. Enter the same counts here to check the total before you write it on the slip.
