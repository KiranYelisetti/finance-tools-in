Understanding home loans in India

A home loan (also called a housing loan or mortgage) is a secured loan from a bank or housing finance company to buy, build or renovate a home. The property is the security, and you repay through equal monthly instalments (EMIs) over 5 to 30 years. Each EMI is part interest and part principal. Early on most of it is interest. In the default example above, the first year's EMIs pay about ₹4.2 lakh of interest and only about ₹1 lakh of principal.

How home loan interest rates are set

Most floating-rate home loans from banks are linked to an external benchmark, usually the RBI repo rate. Your rate is that benchmark plus a spread the bank fixes when it sanctions the loan. Since 1 October 2019 the RBI has required banks to link new floating-rate retail loans to an external benchmark, and the rate must be reset at least once every three months.

When the repo rate changes, your EMI or your remaining tenure changes at the next reset. The spread normally stays as sanctioned, which is why a lower spread is the most valuable thing to negotiate. Housing finance companies are not covered by this rule and usually price loans off their own benchmark rate.

Your credit score, the loan-to-value ratio, the loan amount and your type of employment all affect the spread you are offered. Lenders change their rates often, so use a current quote from the lender rather than any published table.

In the new regime there is no deduction for the home you live in. For a home you let out, the interest is deducted from the rent you receive in either regime. If you are in the 30% slab under the old regime and claim the full ₹2,00,000 of interest and ₹1,50,000 of principal, your tax falls by up to ₹1,09,200 a year (31.2% with the 4% cess, before any surcharge). What to compare between home loan offers

What to checkWhy it matters
Rate typeFloating rates move with the lender's benchmark. Fixed rates stay put but usually start higher, and some fixed loans convert to floating after a few years.
Spread over the benchmarkFor most floating loans the spread is fixed at sanction, so it decides what you pay for the whole tenure. Compare spreads, not just today's headline rate.
Reset frequencyBank loans on an external benchmark reset at least once every three months, so repo rate cuts and rises reach you quickly.
Processing feeUsually a percentage of the loan or a flat fee, with GST on top. It is sometimes waived during offers, so ask.
Prepayment chargesRBI rules do not allow lenders to charge prepayment or foreclosure penalties on floating-rate loans taken by individuals for non-business purposes. Fixed-rate loans can carry a charge, so read the sanction letter.
Bundled insuranceCheck whether a loan protection insurance premium has been added to the loan amount. Ask for the quote without it so you compare like with like.
Total costPut each offer's rate, tenure and fees into this calculator and compare the total interest, not the EMI alone.

General guidance as of November 2026. Confirm current rates and charges with the lender, and see the RBI's website for the rules on loan pricing.

Documents lenders usually ask for

Salaried

  • Identity proof (Aadhaar, PAN or passport)
  • Address proof (utility bill or rent agreement)
  • Last 3 months' salary slips
  • Last 6 months' bank statement
  • Form 16 for the last 2 years

Self-employed

  • Identity and address proof
  • Business proof (GST or registration certificate)
  • ITR for the last 3 years with the computation of income
  • Last 12 months' bank statement
  • Balance sheet and profit and loss statement, certified by a CA

These are estimates from the figures you enter. Your lender's sanction letter and amortisation schedule are what count, and tax rules depend on your full return. This page is for information, not financial or tax advice.
